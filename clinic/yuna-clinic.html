Seasonal vaccines

Flu shots

The CDC recommends a yearly flu vaccine for everyone 6 months and older. This season's vaccines are trivalent, covering two influenza A strains and one B strain. Protection builds over about two weeks, so October and November are the best months to get yours.

AGES 6 MONTHS – 64

Standard-dose flu shot

The everyday vaccine for children and adults. One injection in the upper arm, then a 15-minute observation.

IV hydration therapy

IV menu

Each drip is 500 mL or 1000 mL of IV fluid with added vitamins or minerals, given in a reclining chair. A nurse checks your blood pressure and history first and stays nearby for the whole infusion.

Add-ons to any drip: extra vitamin C (+$25), glutathione push (+$35), anti-nausea medication (+$30), B12 intramuscular shot (+$25, also available on its own).

A flu shot takes about 25 minutes door to door. An IV visit takes 45 to 75 minutes depending on the drip.

    Bring photo ID and your insurance card. Fill in a short health form on a tablet.

    ~5 min

Questions

FAQ

Can I get a flu shot if I'm feeling sick?

A mild cold without fever is fine. If you have a fever or moderate illness, wait until you feel better. Our nurse will check your temperature before the shot.

Can I get a flu shot and a COVID-19 vaccine on the same day?

Yes. The CDC says both can be given at the same visit, usually one in each arm. Ask us which COVID-19 vaccine we have in stock.

Who should not get IV therapy?

IV hydration isn't right for everyone. We may decline or ask for your doctor's approval if you have heart failure, kidney disease, uncontrolled high blood pressure, or are pregnant. The nurse screening covers this.

Does insurance cover IV drips?

No. IV wellness drips are self-pay. Many HSA and FSA plans accept them; we can give you an itemized receipt.

What should I do before an IV?

Eat a light meal and drink water beforehand so your veins are easier to find. Wear a top with sleeves that roll up above the elbow.
